## Results

### 2026-11-08 — pathway diagnostic upgrade

Added an analysis-only pathway diagnostic and rendered it for the 150-ns
fixed-charge run `reference_6h_legacy_4776599` at cutoff 20. It now plots:

- bare-fluxonium instantaneous populations `g0`, `e0`, `f0`, and `g1`, where
  the fluxonium labels are recomputed at the pulse's current external phase;
- the previous fixed-idle-basis populations, retained only as a reference;
- basis-independent cavity photon number and density-matrix trace.

The selected instantaneous-state sum is not required to be one: it contains
only four states, not the full fluxonium-cavity Hilbert space. The trace stays
one, so a deficit in the selected sum is unplotted population rather than lost
probability.

For this pulse, cavity occupation is appreciable mainly in the earlier part of
the protocol and becomes near zero late in the pulse. The sharp late `g0/e0`
exchange remains in the instantaneous diagnostic, so it is not solely an
idle-basis artifact. It appears to be a coherent late-stage maneuver after the
earlier dissipative part; interpretation still needs care before changing the
physics or optimizer.

### 2026-11-08 — declared 150-ns charge-control ablations

Prepared four full-physics, 150-ns local experiments in
`scripts/run_150ns_charge_ablations.sh` (default: 200 generations, population
16, four workers, run sequentially):

1. fixed seeded-random 40-harmonic flux waveform; optimize 20 charge
   corrections;
2. fixed idle flux; optimize 20 charge corrections;
3. joint 40-flux/20-charge search, initialized with random flux coefficients
   and exactly zero charge correction;
4. hold the saved `reference_6h_legacy_4776599` flux waveform fixed; optimize
   20 charge corrections from a seeded random nonzero correction.

The fourth configuration reproduces the parent fixed-charge score
`P(g,0) = 0.995656951626` with a zero charge correction before optimization.
That is the necessary preflight check: any later change is attributable to the
charge block rather than a mismatched held flux waveform.

The random-flux experiment is a single controlled negative-control waveform,
not evidence about all random flux pulses. Repeat it with other declared seeds
only if its result becomes scientifically interesting.

### Future: lock physical waveforms, not coefficient vectors

For a later experiment such as “hold a strong flux pulse fixed while optimizing
charge,” represent the fixed flux control as a saved sampled waveform (for
example, a parent run's `controls.npz`), not as that run's CMA-ES weights.
Weights only have meaning for their exact basis, duration, and harmonic count;
the waveform is the physical quantity we intend to preserve.

The present implementation deliberately accepts a saved waveform only when its
time grid matches exactly. When we resume duration continuation, define and
record an explicit resampling or projection procedure rather than silently
copying an array across durations.
